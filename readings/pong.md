For my own Pong iteration, the first thing I wanted to change was the visual appearance of the game. Since I want to become a game concept artist and have a strong background in drawing, visuals naturally became my starting point. Pong was one of the earliest video games, and I believe its extremely simple, pixelated appearance was largely shaped by the technological limitations of its time. Nowadays, it is much less common to see games with such a simple black-and-white pixelated style.
That being said, I did not want to simply give Pong a visual upgrade. I wanted the new appearance to connect directly to the gameplay and design. So I started thinking about what kind of real-life action resembles the basic mechanic of Pong. Then I thought about something I sometimes do when I cook: when a potato has just been boiled and is still too hot to peel, I toss it back and forth between my hands to cool it down. Isn't that action surprisingly similar to Pong?
That became the starting point for my own iteration. Instead of keeping Pong competitive, I want to turn it into a cooperative game, or possibly even a single-player game. The two hands need to pass the hot potato back and forth for at least three rounds to cool it down completely. Once the potato is successfully cooled, the team earns one point. However, if they drop the potato three times, the game is over.
In this way, I am not simply replacing the paddles and ball with new visuals. The visual concept changes the meaning of the original mechanic as well: instead of two players trying to defeat each other, the two sides are now working together toward the same goal.
