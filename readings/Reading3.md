In this week's reading, we explored what makes a good prototype in game design. One of the ideas that inspired me the most was that a good prototype is not necessarily the most detailed one, but the one that helps designers explore their ideas most effectively. The text explains that a good prototype should leave out unnecessary details without affecting the design experience being explored.
I think deciding what details to leave out is quite subjective. Different levels of simplification can serve different purposes and help designers explore different questions. For example, as mentioned in the article, HCI research has focused heavily on the fidelity of prototypes, since the level of detail directly affects the cost of production. A low-fidelity prototype can be as simple as a few quick sketches on paper. This kind of testing is very cheap and quick to produce, which makes it especially useful in the early stages of game development, when many things are still undecided.
I used this kind of prototyping in my UI design class, and I found it to be a very effective method. However, I had never really thought about how I could apply the same approach to game design. Another reason I like low-fidelity prototyping is that I often get inspired and come up with more ideas while making prototypes. Since they are so quick to create, I can immediately test these new ideas without spending too much time or effort.
On the other hand, high-fidelity prototypes are much closer to the final product. I also worked with this approach in my UI design class. I have to complain that it is extremely time-consuming. Because so much time and effort goes into making these prototypes, I sometimes feel reluctant to make changes, even when I know something could be improved. However, I also think high-fidelity prototypes are very useful when presenting a design to others or conducting interviews and surveys, since they give people a much clearer idea of what the final product might look and feel like.
This article is very useful to me as I am very stuck on my walking stimulator. I struggle to find interesting ideas. I believe what I need to do is brainstorm and try to do some low-fidelity prototypes.
