For this assignment, we were required to build on top of our walking simulator and add two new mechanisms. Firstly, I was able to fix a bug that had been bothering me for a while. Some of my models were flashing, and I found out that it was simply because I had tried to hide some models by toggling the eye icon, believing that this would also make them invisible during gameplay. However, that was not true. Those models were still showing in the game and overlapping with the new models, causing the flashing.

I decided to set my game in an abandoned factory, where the protagonist is trapped in their memories and keeps wandering around. Throughout the game, I wanted to gradually take away the protagonist's senses as a representation of the process of slowly forgetting or letting go of the past.

For my first mechanism, I wanted to combine gameplay with sound. In my original walking simulator project, I had already added many different sound effects. My idea was to use sound as a hint to help the player find diary pages scattered around the map. The closer the player gets to a page, the louder the sound becomes. Once a page is collected, the sound linked to that page will be silenced. When all the pages are collected, the entire scene will become silent. To make the papers more visible to the player, I also made them float up and down by simply coding a sine wave movement.

For my second mechanism, I wanted to design something based on light. After collecting all the diary pages, the player will be required to close all the power switches. I managed to make all the power switches glow with a bright red light before they are closed. However, creating the emissive material was very difficult. My power switches were all imported external models, and at first, I planned to modify their materials directly to make them glow red. I soon found out that I was ruining the baked materials, and everything started to look weird. I eventually found an alternative solution. I created a new red emissive material and attached it to a plane, which worked as an overlay on the original model.

Making the material was also quite problematic. At first, I didn't enable rendering on both sides, so the red glow was only visible when I looked at it from certain angles. Another big issue was that the material looked much more emissive in the Scene view than during actual gameplay. In the Game view, it barely looked like it was glowing at all. I had to follow a YouTube tutorial, and I eventually fixed the issue by enabling Bloom for my gameplay camera. The settings were quite complicated, but I'm glad that everything worked out in the end.

As the player closes each power switch, the screen gradually becomes darker. I achieved this effect by simply adding a black overlay over the entire screen and adjusting its transparency. When all three switches are closed, the screen turns completely black. At first, I tried turning off the lights I had added to the scene, but since the sunlight was still very bright, the effect was barely noticeable. I was very glad that I came up with a simple alternative solution that worked much better.
