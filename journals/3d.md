For this project, we were tasked with creating a 3D walking simulator. This was my first time working with Unity 3D. Since the assignment was about allowing players to freely explore an environment with a particular atmosphere, the first idea that came to my mind was an abandoned factory. Places like this are usually off-limits to the public and can be quite dangerous to explore. I thought it would be an exciting and unusual experience for players to walk through a place they would normally never have the chance to visit.
Abandoned places are often filled with damaged structures, broken equipment, and various obstacles, which made it easier for me to design an interesting level. Instead of having my character move around on a completely flat surface, I was able to create platforms at different heights that players could jump onto, as well as train tracks running below ground level. These changes in elevation added more variety to the environment and made the exploration experience more engaging.
When it came to sound effects, I was initially a bit lost. I wasn't sure what kinds of sounds I should add, since the factory was abandoned and nothing was supposed to be moving. At first, I only had some industrial background noise that created a faint metallic clanging sound. However, while browsing through sound assets, I came across the sound of a train, which immediately gave me an idea: the sounds in my environment didn't necessarily have to follow reality. I could make objects produce the sounds they would have made when the factory was still in operation. These sounds could hint at how busy and lively the place once was, making the abandoned factory feel as though it were filled with memories of its past. I liked the idea of using sound not just to create atmosphere, but also to tell a story about a place that no longer exists as it once did.
During my in-class showcase, my professor pointed out that I needed to pay more attention to the proportions of my environment, something I hadn't really considered while building the scene. My character looked like a giant compared to the surrounding objects. To fix this, I started rescaling my models, using the train door as a reference for the correct proportions. I figured that the player's eye level should be slightly lower than the top of the door to create a more realistic first-person perspective.
However, making these adjustments was not as easy as I expected. Not all of my models were built at the same scale, so I couldn't simply enlarge everything by the same amount. For example, scaling every object by a factor of 1.3 would make some parts of the environment look disproportionate. I had to adjust different models individually and constantly check how they looked in relation to one another. After a lot of trial and error, I finally managed to fix the issue, and the environment now looks much more realistic and believable. I successfully added most of the materials to the model, however, some materials are not transferable from blender, I should keep that in mind that next time when I’m baking the material in blender I should not add too many nodes.
